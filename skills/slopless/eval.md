# Slopless eval

Run after writing or editing. Answer each check pass or fail. Fix every fail and re-run
before returning the draft.

## Factual integrity
1. Zero invented statistics, dollar amounts, dates, credentials, reviews, or testimonials.
2. Every unresolved fact is marked `[VERIFY: ...]`, and none remain if this is going live.
3. Every client quote is verbatim and appears at most once on the page.
4. YMYL claims (legal/medical/financial) are accurate, generally cited, and hedged where the
   subject hedges.

## House style
5. Zero em dashes anywhere.
6. No pronouns for any real person whose pronouns are unconfirmed.
7. On local pages: at least three facts that are true only of this place, and no paragraph
   survives a city find-and-replace.

## Slop patterns
8. No banned buzzwords (delve, leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, transformative, ...).
9. No setup phrases before points ("Here's the thing," "The honest answer:", "Let's be honest").
10. No empty reversals ("It's not X, it's Y") unless the contrast is the actual message.
11. No colon-drama reveals; colons only for lists, labels, quotes.
12. No importance puffery ("testament to," "vital role," "underscores") and no trailing
    "-ing" fake analysis ("highlighting our commitment").
13. No unnamed authority ("experts agree," "studies show") without a named source.
14. No mic-drop closing metaphor and no "In conclusion" recap; the ending is a concrete point
    or a next action.
15. No same-page repetition of a quote, stat, claim, or CTA phrasing.

## Emotional register
16. No templatic empathy ("we understand how stressful..."); any acknowledgment of the
    reader's situation is specific to it.
17. Negative subjects keep their weight: no reflexive "but don't worry" pivot, no bad fact
    neutralized in the same breath.
18. No labeled emotions ("frustrating," "exciting," "stressful" as adjectives doing the
    work); concrete details carry the feeling.

## Readability
19. Every example, analogy, and scenario comes from the reader's world; they would
    recognize the scene from their own week.
20. Every sentence passes the portability test or carries voice.
21. Verbs are direct; no "serves as," "has the ability to," "made a decision."
22. Formatting follows content: no emoji headings, no mid-sentence bold emphasis, no bullet
    lists that should be prose.
23. Sentence lengths vary visibly (some under 8 words, not everything 15 to 25); no two
    adjacent sections share the same structural template.
24. At most one sentence on the page ends in a participial "-ing" tail, and no verbs hide
    inside nominalizations where the plain verb is clearer.
25. Read the opening and closing aloud: both sound like a person who knows the subject.
